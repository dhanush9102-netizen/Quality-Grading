Problem Statement AGR-S05

Grade produce by measuring ratios, not guesses.

Manual quality grading is subjective and changes with the shift. The automated alternative — average the pixel colours, feed a classifier — breaks under ordinary lighting and confidently mislabels anything unusual. AgriGrade fixes the measurement first, then grades with a Random Forest, and shows its work.

YOLOv8 segmentation Class-conditioned features Random Forest · 100 trees Explainable output On-device ready

01 — The problem

Three ways produce grading breaks today

None of these are hard modelling problems. They come from measuring the wrong thing.

Light changes the reading

A channel average is a measurement of the room, not the fruit. Move the crate under a different lamp and the same apple returns a different score.

Unusual fruit is flagged as rotten

A generic classifier trained mostly on red apples reads a brown kiwi as a rotten one. The model is not broken — it was never shown what a healthy kiwi looks like.

A verdict with no receipt

A buyer who is told "Grade B" with no supporting evidence cannot price against it, and a farmer who is wronged has nothing to appeal with.

02 — The idea

Change what you measure, not what you model

A deeper network would not fix the first two problems. A different measurement would.

Average the channels

mean(R, G, B) is an absolute measurement. Multiply every channel by the light level and the number moves with it. The value encodes the room as much as the fruit.

Take the ratio of the channels

(R − G) / (R + G) is dimensionless. Scale every channel by the light level and the scale cancels, because it appears in both the numerator and the denominator. The number survives the move.

One model for every fruit

The model has to learn ten produce classes and every lighting condition from data alone, and it will still guess when a class falls outside its training set.

Route the measurement, then predict

Segment the fruit, read its class, and send it down the recipe that class actually needs. The defence against mislabelling is the feature router, not a bigger network.

Brightness is free. The ratio is exactly invariant to it — the gain cancels top and bottom, so the bar never moves. A colour cast is not free. A warm lamp lifts red and drops blue unevenly, so NDTI drifts a little. Real deployments lean on the mask, a neutral-ish background and reference markers to keep that drift small.

04 — How it works

Four stages, from a phone photo to a priced grade

1

Segment

YOLOv8 segmentation isolates the produce from the background and names its class, so a crate of mixed fruit is handled piece by piece.

mask + ProduceClass
2

Route

The class maps to a colour family, and the family decides which feature recipe runs. This is the step that stops brown produce being scored as rotten red produce.

class → family → recipe
3

Measure

Spectral ratios, texture metrics and a coin-scaled physical measurement run only over the masked foreground, so background and camera distance stop contaminating the result.

feature vector
4

Grade

A 100-tree Random Forest votes, the majority class is the grade, and the mean probability is the confidence. Feature importance comes out with it.

grade + confidence + XAI

06 — The measurement

Four indices, one idea

Every one is a ratio of normalised channels, so the light level cancels. ε keeps a black pixel from dividing by zero.

VARI = (G − R) / (G + R − B + ε)

Chlorophyll density and photosynthetic activity — how green or how alive the surface still is.

NDTI = (R − G) / (R + G + ε)

Anthocyanin and lycopene during ripening. This is the index the demo above moves under a lamp.

YI = (R + G − 2B) / (R + G + B + ε)

Carotenoid progression in bananas, mangoes and papayas — how far along the ripening curve a fruit is.

ExB = 2B − R − G

Excess blue. Grey and white fungal growth on dark or brown skin pushes this up while ripe pigment pushes it down.

Size that does not depend on how far away the phone was

scale (mm/px) = known reference diameter ÷ measured reference diameter

true area (cm²) = pixel area × scale² × 10⁻²

Drop a coin or a 40 mm ArUco tag in the frame and the produce's real area and diameter follow. Without one you still get a grade — just no physical size, and the UI says so rather than inventing a number.

Confidence is the mean vote

Not a softmax on one output — the average of 100 trees' probabilities for the winning class. When the forest splits, the score drops and the UI says the grade is provisional rather than showing a confident-looking bar.

Low confidence is allowed to say so

A number you cannot defend is worse than no number. Below a threshold the result is flagged as provisional, so a marginal batch gets a second capture instead of a wrong price.

08 — Why this works

What the design buys us

Illumination-invariant by construction

Because the indices are ratios, no darkroom, no ring light and no controlled shooting booth. It grades in the packing shed it was given.

Class conditioning kills the brown-fruit bug

Kiwi, chikoo and brown pear get an ExB-and-texture recipe instead of a red-fruit one, so being brown is no longer read as being rotten.

Runs where the produce is

Features via OpenCV, inference via TFLite or ONNX, frames kept on the phone by default. No cloud round trip, no per-batch latency, and nothing retained server-side unless submitted.

Transparent by default

Every grade ships with the features that drove it. A grader who disagrees with the result can see which measurement to argue about.
